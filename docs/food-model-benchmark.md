# Food photo → calories: on-device model benchmark

Run on 2026-10-22/23 on the user's PC (i5-8300H, 16 GB RAM, GTX 1050 Ti 4 GB). Scripts are in [tools/food-bench/](../tools/food-bench/). Raw predictions, manifests and model hashes are in [tools/food-bench/results/2026-10-22/](../tools/food-bench/results/2026-10-22/). Sources are in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Decision

**Put CalorieK on the phone**, exported to ONNX at fp32 (375 MB).
- It had the lowest error on every everyday-food test.
- The gap is statistically clear: the bootstrap 95% confidence interval for the difference excludes zero.
- It is also by far the fastest model.

Calorie Lens is only better on the cafeteria images it was trained on.

**Even the best model is only accurate enough as a starting point.**
- It is off by about 160–190 kcal on a typical meal, roughly 25–35%.
- It **underestimates large portions**. HelloFresh dinners averaging about 770 kcal come out about 170 kcal low.

So in the app, the photo estimate must be shown with a range, must be editable, and should be followed by a quick portion question.

## The question

The user asked for a model fine-tuned specifically for food photo → calories, the most accurate one that is still small enough to run on their phone (Pixel 9). The comparison had to use everyday food photos, not the cafeteria trays most of these models were trained on.

**nutrient-gram-2b was trained differently from how we'd use it.**
- Its training data is 921 HelloFresh photos with ingredient grams **generated by another AI**.
- It was trained with **the recipe text given as an extra input**.
- In our app it only gets the photo, so it is being used outside its training. It was tested anyway, for completeness.

## Test sets

| Set | Images | What it is | Label quality | Why it's included |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **MM-Food-100K** (homemade + restaurant) | 300 (150 + 150) | Real user phone photos | AI or crowd estimates; noisy, about ±10–20% or worse | The closest thing to "photos people actually take" |
| **HelloFresh test split** | 263 | Plated home-cooked dinners, professional photos | HelloFresh's published per-serving nutrition | Real home food with labels that aren't AI guesses. nutrient-gram trained on the *train* split only |
| **Food Portion Benchmark** (val) | 200 | Central Asian dishes, 1–5 per photo, lab setting | **Weighed** grams, but no kcal | A ground-truth test of portion size |
| **Nutrition5k** test, overhead | 100 | US cafeteria plates | **Weighed** | A sanity check: does my pipeline reproduce the published numbers? |

All samples are seeded (seed 2026) and can be reproduced. MM-Food is filtered to 50–1500 kcal with food probability ≥ 0.9.

**Looked for and not used:**
- **[NutritionVerse-Real](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/nutritionverse/nutritionverse-real)** (Kaggle) would be the ideal test: 889 iPhone photos of 251 real meals with every ingredient weighed. But Kaggle requires a signed-in account to download, there's no API token on this PC, and it isn't mirrored anywhere. **Pending your Kaggle token.**
- **[Nutricheck](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/dwiiyy/nutricheck-image-dataset)** (Kaggle) only has per-class nutrition (one number per dish type), so it can't test portions.
- The Kaggle Nutrition5k mirrors are the same data as the official bucket.

## Method

**Safety:**
- I read each model's code before running it.
- Calorie Lens's `model.py` was reviewed (no network access, `exec` or file writes at inference) and is loaded from its exact file path.
- CalorieK's pickle was scanned statically (its only globals are `OrderedDict` and torch tensor rebuilds) and loaded with `weights_only=True`.
- Everything ran in an isolated venv with `python -I`. Downloads are kept away from the scripts.

**Specialist models:**
- Run with PyTorch on the CPU, exactly as their model cards describe.
- Calorie Lens has two readouts: its ingredient-based totals and its direct totals head. The table shows the direct head as "· alt".

**VLMs:**
- llama.cpp b11815 (Vulkan) on the GTX 1050 Ti, Q4_K_M weights with a Q8_0 projector, temperature 0.
- Photos are downscaled to 768 px on the longest side, as an app would do.
- Every model got the same prompt, asking for JSON with items, grams, kcal and macros.
- "· alt" is the kcal computed from the model's listed ingredient grams using Calorie Lens's NutriText table.

**Metrics:**
- MAE, median absolute error, PMAE (MAE ÷ mean true value), bias, share within ±100 kcal, and share within ±25%.
- All models in a table are scored on **the same images**, those every model answered.
- The best-vs-runner-up difference has a paired bootstrap 95% CI (2,000 resamples).

## Results (kcal)

### Everyday phone photos: MM-Food-100K (256 paired images)

| Model | MAE | Median | % error | Bias | Within ±25% | s/img* |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **CalorieK** | **158** | **142** | **36%** | −19 | 39% | 0.46 |
| Calorie Lens (direct head) | 241 | 205 | 54% | +81 | 29% | 4.0 |
| Calorie Lens (ingredients) | 277 | 217 | 63% | +63 | 21% | 4.0 |
| nutrient-gram-2b (NutriText) | 264 | 215 | 60% | −257 | 6% | 4.8 |
| Qwen3-VL-2B (base) | 316 | 150 | 71% | +146 | 55% | 5.2 |

CalorieK beats the runner-up by 83 kcal, 95% CI [−106, −61], so the difference is real.

By group, CalorieK's MAE is 136 for homemade food and 182 for restaurant food.

\* PC timings: specialist models on CPU, VLMs on the GTX 1050 Ti. They are not phone timings.

**One thing in Qwen's favour:**
- Qwen's *median* error (150) is close to CalorieK's, and it lands within ±25% more often (55%).
- But it has huge outliers (MAE 316) and failed to return valid JSON on 8% of photos.
- MM-Food's labels are themselves estimates from a large AI model, so a VLM may agree with them more than the truth warrants.
- On HelloFresh, whose labels are not AI estimates, this advantage disappears (see below).

### Home-cooked dinners: HelloFresh (239 paired images)

| Model | MAE | Median | % error | Bias | Within ±25% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **CalorieK** | **191** | **145** | **24%** | −166 | **60%** |
| Calorie Lens (direct head) | 338 | 316 | 43% | −333 | 20% |
| Qwen3-VL-2B (base) | 374 | 323 | 47% | −335 | 11% |
| Calorie Lens (ingredients) | 409 | 384 | 52% | −407 | 11% |
| nutrient-gram-2b (NutriText) | 604 | 581 | 77% | −604 | 0% |

CalorieK beats the runner-up by 147 kcal, 95% CI [−160, −132], so the difference is real.

**Every model underestimates these large dinners** (median 769 kcal). This is the biggest practical problem.

### Portion weight: Food Portion Benchmark (194 weighed photos)

| Model | Mass MAE | % error | Bias |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Qwen3-VL-2B** | **265 g** | **51%** | −179 g |
| CalorieK | 367 g | 70% | −352 g |
| nutrient-gram-2b | 390 g | 75% | −371 g |
| Calorie Lens | 465 g | 89% | −465 g |

These photos often show 3–5 dishes at once (median 459 g). All the models, which were trained mostly on single plates, badly underestimate them. The general VLM copes best with multi-dish scenes.

### Sanity check: Nutrition5k overhead (88 paired images)

| Model | MAE | % error |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Calorie Lens** | **39** (direct head 36) | **17%** |
| CalorieK | 91 | 40% |
| nutrient-gram-2b | 147 (NutriText) | 65% |
| Qwen3-VL-2B | 193 | 85% |

- Calorie Lens reproduces its published 40.9 kcal / 16.0%, which confirms the pipeline is correct.
- CalorieK's 91 is worse than its published 56 because it was trained on *side-angle* frames, and these photos are *overhead*.

**Conclusion: Calorie Lens is excellent on what it was trained on and fails to generalise. CalorieK generalises best.**

## Converting CalorieK for the phone

| Format | Size | kcal MAE (HelloFresh / MM-Food) | Drift vs PyTorch | s/img (PC CPU) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| PyTorch | 375 MB | 191 / 158 | n/a | 0.46 |
| **ONNX fp32** | **375 MB** | **191 / 158** | 0.1–0.2 kcal | 0.19–0.25 |
| ONNX dynamic int8 | 94 MB | 229 / 176 | 76–121 kcal (max 366) | 0.16–0.18 |
| ONNX fp16 (naive) | 188 MB | n/a: every output is ∞ | overflow | n/a |

- **ONNX fp32** is exact and is what to ship first.
- **Plain int8** costs 19–38 kcal of MAE, so it isn't good enough as is.
- **Naive fp16** overflows. It would need mixed precision (LayerNorm and softmax kept in fp32) or static int8 with calibration. That can come later if the 375 MB download is a problem.
- My simple preprocessing (bilinear resize to 224×224, then (x/255 − 0.5)/0.5) matches the original processor exactly, so it can be ported directly to Kotlin.

### On the Pixel 9 (Android 17), 2026-10-23

ONNX Runtime Android 1.31.0 ran the fp32 model from the app's private storage. I used the debug-only `BenchActivity` on 200 photos: 100 from MM-Food and 100 from HelloFresh.

| Backend | Model load | Inference, median | p90 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **ORT default CPU, 4 threads** | 2.3 s | **0.55 s** | 0.57 s |
| ORT with XNNPACK | 2.0 s | 1.9 s | 2.5 s |

- Decoding and resizing a photo takes a median of 16 ms.
- **Accuracy matches the PC:** MAE is 132 vs 131 kcal on MM-Food and 200 vs 197 kcal on HelloFresh.
- Individual photos differ from the PC by a median of 8–17 kcal (p90 20–65, max 119). Two reasons:
  - Android's resize is different. To approximate PIL's antialiased bilinear resize, the app halves the image step by step first.
  - The phone applies the photo's EXIF rotation, and the PC pipeline didn't.

**Combining models doesn't help.** Averaging CalorieK with Qwen gives 271 / 218 kcal, and the median of three models gives 324 / 164. Neither beats CalorieK alone, so a 1.5 GB second model isn't justified for calories.

## What this means for the app (Phase 2 design)

1. **Show a range, not a single number.** CalorieK's own calibration file gives a 90% interval of ±119 kcal on its test set, but the real-world error is larger. Show "≈ 650 kcal (500–800)".
2. **Ask about portion size after the photo** (small / regular / large / "a full plate"). Every model underestimates big meals. Published research also shows asking cuts the error (CVPR 2026 workshop paper in sources.md).
3. **The result is editable and never saved automatically**, as already planned.
4. **CalorieK gives no food names.** The name comes from what you type, or from your recent foods.
5. **Correct the bias over time (later):** keep your corrections and learn a personal scale factor, since CalorieK runs about 20% low on large meals.

## Caveats

- **Labels:**
  - MM-Food labels are AI or crowd estimates.
  - HelloFresh labels are recipe-computed per-serving values, and its photos are professional.
  - Only the Food Portion Benchmark and Nutrition5k are weighed, and neither is everyday home food.
  - **None of these sets includes Indian or German home cooking.**
- **Prompts:** both VLMs were tested with one prompt each. Their training prompts aren't published. A better prompt might help Qwen a little, but it wouldn't fix its outliers or JSON failures.
- **Phone speed and memory have not been measured yet.** That's the next step: run ONNX Runtime on the Pixel 9.
- The model cards' own claims are self-reported. Our numbers above are independent, but they come from a single run.
